Early-season crop type maps support irrigation planning, production forecasting and food-security monitoring, but classifying crops before the growing season ends is difficult because satellite observations are incomplete and crops of different types look alike in spectral signal. A recent Transformer-based framework combined Sentinel-2 time series with ancillary information and reported that previous-year crop type was the most useful ancillary variable~\cite{genemola2026}. However, that framework appends static information to the time series, and the authors report that under-represented crop classes remain hard to classify~\cite{genemola2026}. We investigate a dual-branch model that processes the satellite time series and static field attributes separately, fuses them with feature-wise linear modulation and cross-attention, and uses a rotation-aware adaptive gate that reduces trust in the previous-year crop when the current-season signal diverges from it. We also investigate a class-balanced focal loss for rare crops.
